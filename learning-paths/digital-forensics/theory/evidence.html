01

What is Digital Evidence?

Core Definition

Digital evidence is information stored or transmitted in digital form that may help establish facts during a forensic investigation. Examples include files, logs, messages, browser records, system artifacts, network captures and metadata.

02

Types of Digital Evidence

Digital evidence can exist in many locations. Investigators identify relevant sources according to the incident, authorized scope and forensic objectives.

📁

Files & Documents

Documents, images, archives and other files may contain information relevant to an investigation.

🧾

System Logs

Logs can provide records of system activity, events, authentication attempts and errors.

🌐

Network Data

Network captures and connection records can help reconstruct communications and activity.

🌍

Browser Artifacts

History, cached resources and other browser artifacts may provide useful investigative context.

⚙

System Artifacts

Operating-system artifacts may reveal configuration, execution and user activity.

🔖

Metadata

Metadata can provide contextual information such as timestamps, file properties and ownership details.

03

Evidence Lifecycle

Digital evidence should be handled through a controlled lifecycle so that investigators can explain what was found, how it was handled and how its integrity was maintained.

STEP 01 Identification

Locate potential evidence sources relevant to the case.

STEP 02 Collection

Acquire evidence using an approved forensic procedure.

STEP 03 Preservation

Protect the original evidence from unnecessary alteration.

STEP 04 Analysis

Examine the evidence and document relevant findings.

04

Evidence Integrity

Evidence integrity means maintaining confidence that the evidence has not been improperly changed after acquisition. Investigators therefore use controlled procedures and verification techniques.

🔐 Integrity Verification

Cryptographic hashes such as SHA-256 can be used to create a reproducible digital fingerprint of a file or forensic image. If the same data is processed again and produces the same hash, the values can be compared as part of an integrity-verification process.

05

Chain of Custody

The chain of custody is a documented history showing who handled evidence, when it was handled, what action was performed and where the evidence was stored or transferred.

Field Purpose
Evidence ID Uniquely identifies the evidence item.
Date & Time Records when an evidence action occurred.
Handler Identifies the person responsible for the item.
Action Documents collection, transfer, analysis or storage.
Location Records where the evidence was stored or transferred.
06

Why Evidence Preservation Matters

Digital evidence can be fragile. Normal system activity may modify timestamps, logs, files or other artifacts. Poor handling can therefore make evidence difficult to interpret or reduce confidence in its authenticity.

A forensic workflow attempts to minimize unnecessary changes and keeps detailed records of actions performed during the investigation.

07

Important Forensic Principles

🔒

Preserve Originals

Protect original evidence and work from verified copies whenever the procedure requires it.

📝

Document Everything

Record relevant actions, timestamps, tools and results so the investigation remains reproducible.

🔍

Verify Findings

Use appropriate verification techniques rather than relying on a single unexplained observation.

08

Authorized Forensic Environment

⚠ CONTROLLED FORENSIC WORKFLOW

Practical forensic activities should be performed only on systems, files and evidence that you own or have explicit authorization to examine. For learning, use CyberNexa training data, intentionally created evidence files, virtual machines and dedicated forensic laboratories.

Forensic Module Summary

Evidence Must Be Preserved, Verified and Documented.

Digital evidence provides investigators with information about events occurring in digital environments. Professional forensic practice depends on proper identification, controlled collection, preservation, integrity verification and a clear chain of custody.

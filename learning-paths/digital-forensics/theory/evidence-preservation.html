01

What is Evidence Preservation?

Evidence preservation is the process of protecting digital evidence from alteration, destruction, contamination or unauthorized access during an investigation.

Digital evidence can include files, emails, browser records, logs, disk images, memory captures, network records and other digital artifacts.

Key Principle: The original evidence should remain unchanged. Investigators should work on verified forensic copies whenever possible.
02

Why Evidence Preservation Matters

Integrity

Preservation helps demonstrate that evidence has not been changed after collection.

Authenticity

Proper documentation helps establish that the evidence is the same evidence originally collected.

Reliability

A controlled preservation process makes forensic findings more reliable and reproducible.

Accountability

Evidence handling records show who accessed, transferred or examined the evidence.

04

Chain of Custody

Chain of custody is the documented history of evidence from the moment it is collected until the investigation is completed.

  • Who collected the evidence?
  • When was it collected?
  • Where was it collected?
  • Who handled or transferred it?
  • Why was it accessed?
  • Where was it stored?
05

Hash Verification

A cryptographic hash produces a fixed-length value representing the contents of digital evidence. Investigators can calculate a hash before and after examination to check whether the contents changed.

06

Evidence Preservation Best Practices

  • Do not modify the original evidence unnecessarily.
  • Use forensic copies for examination whenever possible.
  • Calculate and record cryptographic hashes.
  • Maintain a complete chain-of-custody record.
  • Document dates, times and actions.
  • Restrict access to authorized personnel.
  • Store evidence in a controlled environment.
  • Keep evidence records accurate and complete.
07

Simple Investigation Example

Suppose an investigator collects a laptop suspected of containing evidence of a security incident.

The investigator documents the device, creates a forensic image, calculates its SHA-256 hash and records the hash in the evidence documentation.

The forensic copy is then examined instead of directly modifying the original device. If the evidence is transferred to another authorized investigator, that transfer is also recorded.

Goal: Preserve the original state of evidence and maintain a traceable record of every important handling event.

Topic Summary

Evidence preservation protects digital evidence from alteration or loss. The major concepts include controlled collection, forensic copies, hashing, chain of custody, documentation and secure storage. These practices help investigators maintain the integrity and reliability of digital evidence.

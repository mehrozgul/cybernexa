01

What is Browser Forensics?

Forensic Definition

Browser forensics is the process of examining browser artifacts to identify and interpret information about web activity. Investigators may analyze artifacts such as browsing history, cached resources, downloads, bookmarks, cookies and related timestamps.

02

Why Browser Forensics Matters

Web browsers can leave multiple artifacts on a system. These artifacts may help investigators understand what activity occurred and when it occurred.

ARTIFACT 01

Browsing History

Records of previously visited web resources can provide useful investigative context.

ARTIFACT 02

Downloads

Download records can help establish relationships between websites, files and timestamps.

ARTIFACT 03

Cookies

Cookie records can provide information about websites and browser interactions.

ARTIFACT 04

Cache

Cached resources may contain remnants of previously accessed web content.

ARTIFACT 05

Bookmarks

Saved pages may reveal useful information about frequently referenced resources.

ARTIFACT 06

Timestamps

Time-related information can help investigators construct an activity timeline.

03

Common Browser Artifacts

History

Helps identify previously visited pages and associated browsing timestamps.

Downloads

Can connect downloaded files with the web resources from which they originated.

Cookies

Small browser records associated with websites and stored browser state.

Cache

Locally stored web resources that may provide additional investigative evidence.

Bookmarks

User-saved web resources that may provide investigative context.

Browser Sessions

Session-related information may help explain browser activity and state.

04

Browser Forensics Process

Investigators should follow a structured process so that browser artifacts remain reliable and the conclusions are supported by evidence.

01
Identify the Evidence Source Determine which browser profile, system or forensic image contains the relevant artifacts.
02
Preserve the Evidence Avoid modifying the original evidence and maintain appropriate forensic handling.
03
Acquire Relevant Artifacts Collect appropriate browser artifacts from the approved forensic source.
04
Analyze the Data Examine URLs, timestamps, downloads, cookies, cache records and other relevant information.
05
Correlate Evidence Compare browser artifacts with other evidence sources to establish a consistent timeline.
06
Document Findings Record observations, timestamps, evidence references and conclusions clearly.
05

Browser Artifacts & Incident Timeline

One of the important purposes of browser forensics is to place individual artifacts into a chronological sequence.

Example Timeline

09:10 — A website is visited.
09:13 — A file is downloaded.
09:16 — Another web resource is accessed.
09:20 — Browser activity stops.

Investigators should not treat one artifact as absolute proof by itself. Multiple evidence sources should be correlated before reaching a conclusion.

06

Important Forensic Considerations

⚠ Evidence Must Be Interpreted Carefully

Browser artifacts can be deleted, modified, expired, overwritten or affected by browser privacy settings. Therefore, investigators should correlate browser evidence with other sources such as system logs, network evidence and file-system artifacts.

Module Summary

Browser Activity Leaves Investigative Evidence

Browser forensics focuses on identifying, preserving and analyzing browser artifacts such as history, downloads, cookies, cache, bookmarks and timestamps. The objective is to reconstruct activity using reliable evidence and proper forensic methodology.

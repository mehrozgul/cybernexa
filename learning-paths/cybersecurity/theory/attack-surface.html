Attack Surface

Learn what an attack surface is, what makes up an organization's attack surface, why it matters and how security teams reduce unnecessary exposure.

What is an Attack Surface?

An attack surface is the collection of all possible points where an attacker could attempt to gain unauthorized access to a system, application, network or organization.

Why Attack Surface Matters

Every exposed system, service, account or application can create a possible entry point for an attacker. Understanding the attack surface helps security teams identify and reduce unnecessary exposure.

  • Helps identify exposed systems and services.
  • Helps security teams prioritize security controls.
  • Reduces unnecessary entry points for attackers.
  • Supports vulnerability management and risk reduction.

Main Components of an Attack Surface

🌐

Network

Internet-facing servers, routers, firewalls, ports and network services can become part of the attack surface.

💻

Devices

Computers, laptops, mobile devices and other connected endpoints can provide possible attack points.

🌍

Web Applications

Websites, APIs and web applications may expose services that attackers can attempt to abuse.

👤

User Accounts

User accounts, administrator accounts and authentication systems can become targets.

☁️

Cloud Resources

Cloud servers, storage, databases and exposed cloud services can increase an organization's attack surface.

🔌

Services & Ports

Open ports and running services can provide network-accessible points that require appropriate security controls.

Types of Attack Surface

Digital Attack Surface

The digital attack surface includes internet-facing systems, applications, APIs, cloud resources, accounts, devices, ports and network services.

Physical Attack Surface

The physical attack surface includes physical devices, servers, offices, access points and other assets that an attacker may physically interact with.

Simple Example

Example: Company Web Server

Suppose a company has a public website, an exposed web server, several open services and employee accounts. These exposed assets form part of the company's attack surface. Security teams can reduce exposure by closing unnecessary services, updating software, securing accounts and limiting public access.

How to Reduce Attack Surface

  • Remove unnecessary services and applications.
  • Close unnecessary network ports.
  • Keep operating systems and applications updated.
  • Use strong authentication and MFA.
  • Apply least-privilege access.
  • Monitor exposed systems and network activity.
  • Regularly identify and assess vulnerabilities.

Key Concepts

🎯

Exposure

The amount of a system that is accessible or visible to potential attackers.

🛡️

Attack Surface Reduction

The process of removing or securing unnecessary entry points to reduce security exposure.

Summary

An attack surface represents the possible entry points an attacker may target. It can include networks, devices, applications, accounts, cloud resources and services. Security teams reduce risk by identifying exposed assets, removing unnecessary exposure and applying appropriate security controls.

CYBERSECURITY FUNDAMENTALS

Authentication

Learn how authentication verifies a user's identity, understand common authentication factors and discover how secure authentication protects accounts and systems.

What is Authentication?

Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user, device or system before allowing access to a protected resource.

Why Authentication Matters

Authentication helps ensure that only verified users or systems can access protected accounts, applications, networks and data.

  • Protects user accounts from unauthorized access.
  • Helps secure sensitive information.
  • Verifies the identity of users and systems.
  • Provides the first security layer before authorization.

Basic Authentication Process

1

Identification

The user claims an identity, usually by providing a username, email address or account identifier.

2

Credential Submission

The user provides authentication information such as a password, security key or biometric factor.

3

Verification

The system checks the supplied credentials against the information stored or managed by the service.

4

Access Decision

If authentication succeeds, the system can move to authorization and determine what the user may access.

Authentication Factors

🔑

Something You Know

Information known by the user, such as a password, PIN or security answer.

📱

Something You Have

A physical or digital item controlled by the user, such as a security key or authenticator device.

👆

Something You Are

A biometric characteristic such as a fingerprint or facial characteristic.

📍

Contextual Factors

Additional contextual information such as device, location or behavioral signals may help support authentication decisions.

Common Authentication Methods

  • Username and password authentication.
  • One-time passwords (OTP).
  • Authentication applications.
  • Hardware security keys.
  • Biometric authentication.
  • Certificate-based authentication.

Authentication vs Authorization

Authentication

Authentication answers: “Who are you?”

Authorization

Authorization answers: “What are you allowed to access?”

Simple Example

When you log in to an online banking account, the system first authenticates your identity. After that, authorization determines which banking operations your account is permitted to perform.

Secure Authentication Practices

  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
  • Never share authentication credentials.
  • Protect recovery methods and security keys.
  • Use secure authentication protocols.
  • Monitor suspicious login activity.
  • Apply account lockout or rate-limiting controls where appropriate.

Simple Example

Example: Email Account Login

A user enters their email address and password. The email service verifies the credentials. If multi-factor authentication is enabled, the user may also need to approve a sign-in or provide another authentication factor before access is granted.

Key Concepts

🔐

Credentials

Information or mechanisms used to prove an identity during authentication.

🛡️

Identity Verification

The process of checking whether the claimed identity can be trusted.

🔑

Authentication Factor

A category of evidence used to verify identity, such as knowledge, possession or inherence.

🚪

Access Control

Security mechanisms that control access to protected resources after identity verification.

Summary

Authentication verifies the identity of a user, device or system before access is provided. Common authentication factors include something you know, something you have and something you are. Strong authentication, especially when combined with MFA, helps protect accounts and sensitive resources from unauthorized access.

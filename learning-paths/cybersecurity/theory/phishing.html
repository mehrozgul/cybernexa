Phishing

Learn what phishing is, how attackers use deceptive messages to manipulate users, the common types of phishing and how users can recognize and prevent phishing attacks.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering technique in which an attacker uses deceptive messages, websites or other communication methods to trick people into revealing sensitive information, clicking unsafe links or performing an unwanted action.

Why Phishing Matters

Phishing targets people rather than relying only on technical vulnerabilities. A convincing message can cause a user to disclose credentials, open a malicious attachment or visit a fraudulent website.

  • Can lead to account compromise.
  • Can expose sensitive information.
  • Can be used as an initial access technique.
  • Can affect individuals and organizations.

Common Types of Phishing

📧

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to appear as if they were sent by a trusted person or organization.

🎯

Spear Phishing

A targeted phishing attempt designed for a specific person or organization.

📱

Smishing

Phishing performed through SMS or other text messaging services.

☎️

Vishing

Phishing performed through voice calls or other telephone-based communication.

👔

Whaling

A targeted phishing attack aimed at senior or high-value individuals in an organization.

🔗

Fake Websites

Fraudulent websites designed to imitate legitimate services and deceive users.

How a Phishing Attack Works

1. Deception

The attacker creates a message or communication designed to look trustworthy.

2. User Interaction

The victim may be encouraged to click a link, open an attachment, provide information or perform another action.

3. Information or Access

If the victim follows the deceptive instructions, sensitive information or account access may be exposed.

4. Security Impact

The compromised information may then be used for account compromise, fraud, further social engineering or other security incidents.

Common Warning Signs

  • Unexpected requests for passwords or sensitive information.
  • Urgent or threatening language.
  • Suspicious links or unfamiliar domains.
  • Unexpected attachments.
  • Sender address that does not match the claimed organization.
  • Requests for unusual payments or account actions.
  • Messages that create pressure to act immediately.

Simple Example

Example: Fake Account Alert

A user receives a message claiming that their online account will be suspended unless they immediately verify their information. The message contains a link to a website that looks similar to the legitimate service. Instead of following the link, the user should independently open the official service and verify whether the alert is genuine.

How to Prevent Phishing

  • Verify unexpected requests through an independent channel.
  • Check the sender and domain carefully.
  • Avoid clicking suspicious links.
  • Do not open unexpected attachments.
  • Use multi-factor authentication.
  • Keep operating systems and applications updated.
  • Report suspicious messages to the appropriate security team.

Key Concepts

⚠️

Social Engineering

Manipulating people into performing actions that may compromise security.

🔍

Verification

Confirming a request independently before providing information or taking action.

🌐

Suspicious Domain

A website address that differs from the legitimate domain or appears unusual.

🛡️

MFA

An additional authentication factor that can reduce the impact of stolen passwords.

Summary

Phishing is a deceptive social engineering technique used to trick users into revealing information or performing unsafe actions. Common forms include email phishing, spear phishing, smishing and vishing. Users can reduce risk by checking messages carefully, verifying unexpected requests, avoiding suspicious links and attachments, using MFA and reporting suspicious activity.

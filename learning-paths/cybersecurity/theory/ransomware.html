MALWARE FUNDAMENTALS

Ransomware

Learn what ransomware is, how it affects systems, how ransomware incidents can impact organizations and how security teams can reduce ransomware risk.

What is Ransomware?

Ransomware is a type of malicious software that prevents users from accessing files or systems, commonly by encrypting data, and then demands payment from the victim in exchange for restoring access or providing a decryption key.

Why Ransomware Matters

Ransomware can affect individuals, businesses, educational institutions and other organizations. A successful ransomware incident may make important files unavailable and can interrupt normal operations.

  • Can make important files inaccessible.
  • Can interrupt business and organizational operations.
  • Can cause financial and recovery costs.
  • Can affect the availability of critical systems.

Main Characteristics of Ransomware

🔒

File Encryption

Many ransomware attacks encrypt files so that the victim cannot normally access their data.

💰

Ransom Demand

Attackers may demand payment in exchange for restoring access to affected data.

📁

Data Unavailability

Important documents, databases and other files may become unavailable during an attack.

🖥️

System Disruption

Ransomware can disrupt normal system and organizational operations.

🌐

Network Impact

In some incidents, ransomware can affect multiple connected systems across a network.

⚠️

Security Risk

Ransomware incidents can create significant availability, recovery and data-security risks.

Common Types of Ransomware

Crypto Ransomware

Crypto ransomware focuses on encrypting files and making data inaccessible until the victim receives a means of restoring access.

Locker Ransomware

Locker ransomware focuses on preventing access to a device or system, which can stop the user from normally using the system.

Double-Extortion Ransomware

In double-extortion incidents, attackers may both encrypt data and threaten to publish or leak stolen information if their demands are not met.

How Ransomware Can Reach Systems

Ransomware can reach systems through different initial-access methods. Common examples include malicious email attachments, compromised credentials, vulnerable services and malicious downloads.

  • Malicious or unexpected email attachments.
  • Compromised user credentials.
  • Exploitation of vulnerable systems or services.
  • Malicious downloads or software.
  • Compromised remote-access services.

Simple Example

Example: Ransomware in a Company

Suppose an employee opens a malicious attachment received through email. The malware executes on the computer and important company files become inaccessible. The attacker then displays a ransom message demanding payment. The security team must isolate affected systems, investigate the incident and restore data using safe backups where available.

How to Reduce Ransomware Risk

  • Maintain regular and tested backups.
  • Keep operating systems and applications updated.
  • Use strong passwords and multi-factor authentication.
  • Train users to recognize suspicious emails and files.
  • Apply least-privilege access.
  • Use endpoint and network security controls.
  • Monitor unusual system and network activity.
  • Segment important systems and networks where appropriate.

What to Do During a Ransomware Incident

  • Isolate affected systems from the network.
  • Report the incident to the responsible security team.
  • Preserve relevant logs and evidence.
  • Identify affected systems and accounts.
  • Remove the underlying threat before restoration.
  • Restore systems from trusted and verified backups when available.

Key Concepts

🔐

Encryption

Ransomware commonly uses encryption to make targeted files inaccessible to the victim.

💾

Backup

Reliable, tested backups can support recovery after ransomware affects production systems.

🛡️

Prevention

Patching, MFA, security awareness and access controls can reduce ransomware risk.

🚨

Incident Response

Fast isolation, investigation and controlled recovery are important during ransomware incidents.

Summary

Ransomware is malicious software that can prevent access to files or systems, commonly through data encryption, and may demand payment from victims. Organizations can reduce risk through backups, patching, MFA, least privilege, security awareness, monitoring and effective incident-response procedures.

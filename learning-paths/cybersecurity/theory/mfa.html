Multi-Factor Authentication

Learn how Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) adds additional verification factors to protect accounts against unauthorized access.

What is Multi-Factor Authentication?

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) is a security mechanism that requires a user to provide two or more different authentication factors before access is granted to a system or account.

Why MFA Matters

Passwords can be stolen, guessed or exposed through phishing and other attacks. MFA adds another verification step, making unauthorized account access more difficult even when a password is compromised.

  • Adds an additional layer of account protection.
  • Helps reduce the impact of stolen passwords.
  • Provides stronger identity verification.
  • Helps protect sensitive accounts and systems.

Main Authentication Factors

🔑

Something You Know

Information known by the user, such as a password, PIN or security answer.

📱

Something You Have

A physical or digital object possessed by the user, such as a security key or authenticator device.

👆

Something You Are

A biometric characteristic such as a fingerprint or facial feature.

📍

Contextual Factors

Additional signals such as device, location or login context may be used by some authentication systems.

MFA vs 2FA

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

2FA specifically uses two authentication factors from different categories.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA is the broader concept of using multiple authentication factors. Two-factor authentication is therefore a specific type of MFA.

Simple Example

Example: Online Account Login

A user enters a password and then confirms the login using an authenticator application or another approved verification factor. The account therefore requires more than just the password before access is granted.

Benefits of MFA

  • Provides additional protection beyond passwords.
  • Helps reduce account takeover risk.
  • Protects important applications and services.
  • Strengthens identity verification.
  • Adds protection when passwords are compromised.

MFA Security Best Practices

  • Enable MFA on important accounts.
  • Prefer strong phishing-resistant authentication methods where supported.
  • Protect authenticator devices and security keys.
  • Never approve unexpected authentication requests.
  • Keep recovery methods secure and up to date.

Key Concepts

🛡️

Additional Verification

MFA requires additional evidence of identity beyond a single password.

🔐

Account Protection

MFA helps protect accounts when a password alone is no longer sufficient.

Summary

Multi-Factor Authentication adds multiple verification factors to the login process. Common factors include something you know, something you have and something you are. MFA provides an additional security layer and helps reduce the impact of compromised passwords.

What is Eradication?

Eradication is the incident response phase in which security teams remove the underlying threat, malicious artifacts and attacker persistence from affected systems.

Definition

First, understand the core meaning of Eradication.

Core Definition

Eradication is the process of completely removing the cause of a cybersecurity incident, including malware, malicious files, unauthorized access, attacker persistence and other identified threats from affected systems.

How Eradication Works

Security teams remove the threat after understanding the incident and containing its impact.

Eradication normally begins after the security team has investigated the incident and identified the threat responsible for the compromise.

The team identifies malicious software, unauthorized accounts, persistence mechanisms, compromised credentials, vulnerable components and other artifacts associated with the incident.

Appropriate remediation actions are then performed. These may include removing malware, deleting malicious files, disabling compromised accounts, resetting affected credentials, removing unauthorized persistence and patching exploited vulnerabilities.

The objective is to ensure that the attacker or malicious software cannot continue using the compromised environment. Systems should be checked after remediation before they are returned to normal operation.

Why Eradication Matters

Removing only the visible symptoms of an incident may allow the threat to return.

REASON 01

Remove the Threat

Eradication removes malware, malicious files and other identified components responsible for the incident.

REASON 02

Stop Persistence

Security teams remove mechanisms that could allow an attacker or malware to regain access.

REASON 03

Prevent Recurrence

Fixing exploited vulnerabilities and compromised credentials helps reduce the chance of the same incident happening again.

Simple Example

Consider a workstation infected with malware.

🚨 Identified Threat

Investigation shows that malware was installed on a workstation and created a mechanism that allowed it to start again after the system was restarted.

✓ Eradication

The security team removes the malicious software, removes the identified persistence mechanism, checks related artifacts and verifies that the threat is no longer present.

Key Concepts

Important terms to remember for this topic.

Eradication: Removing the underlying threat and its associated malicious artifacts.
Malware: Malicious software designed to perform harmful or unauthorized activities.
Persistence: A mechanism that allows an attacker or malware to maintain or regain access to a system.
Remediation: Actions taken to correct the conditions that caused or enabled the security incident.
Credential Reset: Changing credentials that may have been compromised during an incident.
Verification: Checking systems after remediation to confirm that the identified threat has been removed.

Summary

Eradication is the incident response phase focused on removing the underlying cause of a security incident. It can involve removing malware, deleting malicious artifacts, eliminating attacker persistence, disabling compromised accounts, resetting affected credentials and fixing exploited vulnerabilities. The objective is to remove the threat and verify that it can no longer continue affecting the environment.

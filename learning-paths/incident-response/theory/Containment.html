What is Containment?

Containment is the process of limiting the spread and impact of a cybersecurity incident by isolating affected systems, accounts, networks or other resources.

Definition

First, understand the core meaning of Containment.

Core Definition

Containment is the Incident Response phase in which security teams take actions to prevent a security incident from spreading or causing additional damage while the investigation and remediation continue.

How Containment Works

Containment focuses on controlling the incident before the threat can cause further damage.

After an incident has been identified and assessed, security teams determine which systems, accounts or network resources may be affected.

The team then applies appropriate containment actions. For example, a compromised computer may be isolated from the network, or a compromised account may be disabled or temporarily restricted.

Containment does not necessarily remove the attacker or malware immediately. Its primary purpose is to limit the incident's scope and prevent additional damage.

Once the incident is controlled, security teams can continue with eradication, recovery and further investigation.

Why Containment Matters

Containment helps security teams control an active incident while protecting additional systems and data.

REASON 01

Stop the Spread

Containment can prevent an attacker, malware or compromised account from affecting additional systems.

REASON 02

Limit Damage

Isolating affected resources can reduce the potential impact on systems, services and data.

REASON 03

Protect Investigation

Controlled containment can help security teams continue investigation while reducing ongoing attacker activity.

Types of Containment

Containment can be performed for a short period or maintained for a longer period depending on the incident.

Short-Term Containment

Short-term containment provides an immediate response to limit an active incident. Examples include isolating a compromised device, disabling a suspicious account or blocking malicious network traffic.

Long-Term Containment

Long-term containment provides continued protection while the security team performs deeper investigation, eradication and recovery activities.

Simple Example

Consider a compromised workstation as an example.

🚨 Possible Incident

A workstation shows suspicious network activity and security monitoring indicates that malware may have compromised the system.

✓ Containment

The security team isolates the workstation from the network while preserving the system for investigation. Other systems continue operating while the affected device is analyzed.

Key Concepts

Important terms to remember for this topic.

Containment: Actions taken to limit the scope and impact of an incident.
Isolation: Separating an affected resource from other systems or networks.
Short-Term: Immediate actions used to control an active incident.
Long-Term: Continued controls used while deeper investigation and remediation occur.
Compromised System: A system that may have been affected or controlled by a threat.
Incident Scope: The systems, accounts, data or resources affected by an incident.

Summary

Containment is an important Incident Response phase used to control a cybersecurity incident and limit its impact. Security teams identify affected resources, assess the risk and apply appropriate isolation or blocking measures. Containment may be short-term or long-term depending on the situation. After the threat is controlled, teams can continue with eradication, recovery and investigation.

Incident Triage Training

What is Triage?

Incident Triage is the process of quickly evaluating a security incident to understand its nature, severity, scope and priority so that the appropriate response can begin.

Definition

First, understand the core meaning of Incident Triage.

Core Definition

Incident Triage is the process of evaluating and prioritizing a cybersecurity incident by examining available information, determining its severity and scope, and deciding how urgently it requires a response.

How Incident Triage Works

Security teams use triage to quickly understand an incident before deciding what response actions are required.

When a security alert is generated, analysts first collect the available information about the event.

They examine details such as the affected user, device, system, network activity, time of occurrence and type of security event.

The analyst then evaluates the potential impact and determines whether the alert represents a real security incident or a possible false positive.

If the event is confirmed as an incident, it is prioritized according to factors such as severity, affected assets, business impact and urgency.

Why Incident Triage Matters

Triage helps security teams focus their resources on incidents that require attention and response.

REASON 01

Prioritize Incidents

Triage helps determine which incidents require immediate attention and which can be handled later.

REASON 02

Reduce Response Time

Quick evaluation allows security teams to begin the appropriate response without unnecessary delay.

REASON 03

Use Resources Effectively

Triage helps analysts and security teams allocate their time and resources according to incident priority.

Incident Priority Levels

Incidents can be prioritized according to their potential impact and urgency.

High Priority

A potentially serious incident that may affect critical systems, sensitive information or important business operations. It normally requires rapid investigation and response.

Medium Priority

An incident that requires investigation and response but does not appear to create an immediate critical impact.

Low Priority

A lower-impact event that can normally be investigated according to available resources and operational priorities.

Simple Example

Consider a suspicious login alert as an example of triage.

Security Alert

A monitoring system reports multiple failed login attempts followed by a successful login from an unusual location.

Triage Decision

The analyst checks the account, login time, source location, device information and related activity. If the activity appears suspicious, the incident receives an appropriate priority for further investigation.

Key Concepts

Important terms to remember for Incident Triage.

Alert: A notification generated by a security monitoring or detection system.
Validation: Checking whether an alert represents a real security event or a possible false positive.
Severity: An assessment of how serious the potential security impact is.
Scope: The systems, accounts, devices or data potentially affected by an incident.
Priority: The level of urgency assigned to an incident.
Escalation: Passing an incident to the appropriate security personnel when additional expertise or response authority is required.

Summary

Incident Triage is an important part of Incident Response. It helps security teams evaluate alerts, collect relevant information, validate suspicious activity, understand the potential scope and impact, and assign an appropriate priority. Effective triage allows security teams to focus their resources on incidents that require timely investigation and response.

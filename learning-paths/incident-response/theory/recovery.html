Incident Recovery Training

What is Recovery?

Recovery is the Incident Response phase in which affected systems, services and data are restored to normal and secure operation after a cybersecurity incident.

RECOVERY COMMAND ● RESTORING
Status Restoration
Category Incident Response
Priority Business Continuity
Goal Normal Operation

Definition

First, understand the core meaning of Recovery in Incident Response.

Core Definition

Recovery is the process of restoring affected systems, applications, networks and data to normal and secure operation after a cybersecurity incident.

How Recovery Works

Recovery focuses on safely returning affected systems and services to normal operation.

After the threat has been removed during eradication, security teams must restore the affected systems and services.

The recovery process may involve restoring data from clean backups, rebuilding compromised systems, reinstalling applications and reconnecting systems to the network.

Before returning a system to normal operation, security teams verify that the original threat has been removed and that the system is functioning safely.

Recovery also includes monitoring restored systems for signs of repeated or related malicious activity.

Why Recovery Matters

Proper recovery helps organizations safely resume normal business and IT operations.

REASON 01

Restore Services

Recovery helps return affected applications, systems and services to normal operation.

REASON 02

Restore Data

Clean and verified backups can be used to recover important data affected by an incident.

REASON 03

Prevent Repeated Impact

Monitoring and validation help ensure that restored systems remain secure after recovery.

Simple Example

Consider a compromised server as an example.

️ Affected System

A server was compromised during a cybersecurity incident. The malicious software has already been removed and the system is ready for restoration.

Recovery

The security team restores the server from a clean backup, verifies its configuration, reconnects it carefully and monitors the system for suspicious activity.

Key Concepts

Important terms to remember for this topic.

Restoration: Returning affected systems or services to operational condition.
Backup: A stored copy of data used to restore information when needed.
Validation: Checking that a restored system is working correctly and securely.
Reconnection: Carefully returning a recovered system to the production network.
Monitoring: Observing restored systems for suspicious or abnormal activity.
Business Continuity: Maintaining or restoring important business operations after disruption.

Summary

Recovery is the Incident Response phase that focuses on restoring affected systems, services and data to normal and secure operation. It may involve restoring clean backups, rebuilding systems, validating their security, reconnecting them to the network and continuously monitoring them. The main goal of Recovery is to safely resume normal operations while reducing the chance of another incident.

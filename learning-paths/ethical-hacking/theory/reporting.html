01

What is Security Reporting?

Core Definition

Security reporting is the process of documenting vulnerabilities, observations, evidence, impact and remediation recommendations discovered during an authorized security assessment. A good report converts technical results into information that both technical teams and decision-makers can understand and act upon.

02

Why Security Reporting Matters

Finding a vulnerability is only one part of a security assessment. Organizations also need accurate evidence, business context and practical remediation guidance.

A structured report creates a clear record of what was tested and what should be improved.

✓
Communicate findings Present technical observations in a clear and understandable format.
✓
Support remediation Provide useful information that helps teams correct identified weaknesses.
✓
Preserve evidence Maintain an accurate record of observations, validation and assessment results.
03

Security Report Structure

A professional security report normally follows a consistent structure. The exact format can vary between organizations, but the following sections provide a useful foundation.

SECTION 01

Executive Summary

Provides a high-level overview of the assessment, major findings and overall security observations.

SECTION 02

Scope

Defines the systems, applications, networks and activities included in the assessment.

SECTION 03

Methodology

Explains the assessment approach and testing process used during the engagement.

SECTION 04

Findings

Documents individual vulnerabilities or security observations discovered during testing.

SECTION 05

Evidence

Includes appropriate evidence supporting the reported finding and its validation.

SECTION 06

Remediation

Provides practical recommendations for reducing or removing the identified security weakness.

04

Anatomy of a Security Finding

🔎
Title A short description that identifies the security issue.
🎯
Affected Asset Identifies the authorized system, application or component affected.
⚠
Impact Explains what security consequences may result if the issue remains unresolved.
🛠
Remediation Describes the recommended defensive action for addressing the finding.
05

Responsible Reporting

Security reports may contain sensitive technical information. Ethical hackers should therefore handle assessment results responsibly and share them only with authorized recipients.

⚠ AUTHORIZED REPORT HANDLING

Use realistic but non-sensitive information in learning exercises. Do not publish credentials, private data, confidential configuration details or other sensitive assessment information. In real engagements, follow the organization's agreed reporting and disclosure procedures.

06

Professional Finding Example

Suppose an authorized training application contains a security weakness. The tester should document the issue in a way that allows the responsible team to understand and reproduce the observation safely.

SAMPLE SECURITY FINDING
Title: Weak Authentication Configuration
Asset: Authorized Training Application
Severity: Medium
Impact: May increase the risk of unauthorized account access.
Recommendation: Strengthen authentication controls and apply appropriate account protection mechanisms.
07

Key Reporting Terms

Finding

A documented security weakness or observation discovered during an assessment.

Evidence

Information that supports the existence or validation of a reported finding.

Severity

An indication of the potential seriousness or security impact of a finding.

Remediation

A recommended action intended to reduce or eliminate the identified security weakness.

Module Summary

Find It. Prove It. Explain It. Fix It.

Security reporting is the final communication stage of ethical hacking. A professional report connects the technical finding with its evidence, impact and remediation. Clear reporting helps organizations understand their security posture and take appropriate defensive action.

01

What is Reconnaissance?

Core Definition

Reconnaissance is the process of collecting information about an authorized target before performing deeper security assessment activities. The collected information can help an ethical hacker understand the target's assets, technologies, public exposure and possible areas that require further investigation.

02

Why Reconnaissance Matters

Security testing becomes more structured when the tester understands what is actually inside the approved scope. Reconnaissance provides an initial information baseline that can guide later assessment activities.

Instead of immediately interacting deeply with a target, an ethical hacker first builds an organized understanding of the environment and identifies information that may be relevant to the assessment.

✓
Understand the Target Identify the systems, services, domains or applications that are included in the approved scope.
✓
Reduce Guesswork Organize known information before moving toward more detailed security testing.
✓
Build an Assessment Baseline Create a clear starting point for later scanning, enumeration and vulnerability assessment.
03

Types of Reconnaissance

Reconnaissance can generally be divided into passive and active approaches. The major difference is whether the tester directly interacts with the target while collecting information.

TYPE 01

Passive Reconnaissance

Information is collected without directly interacting with the target system. Publicly available information is commonly used.

TYPE 02

Active Reconnaissance

The tester interacts directly with an authorized target to obtain additional information. This should only occur within approved scope.

TYPE 03

Hybrid Approach

A professional assessment may combine passive research with controlled active activities according to the engagement rules.

04

Common Information Sources

Reconnaissance may involve several legitimate information sources. In a learning environment, students should use only information and systems that are intentionally provided for security training.

Domain Information

Basic information about approved domain names, subdomains and related services.

DNS Information

DNS records can provide useful information about how approved domains are configured.

Public Documentation

Public documentation, technical pages and other intentionally exposed information may help establish context.

Technology Clues

Publicly visible technology indicators can help identify possible platforms or components.

05

Reconnaissance Workflow

A professional reconnaissance process should be organized and documented. A simple beginner workflow is:

STEP 01

Define Scope

Confirm the exact target, allowed assets, restrictions and testing window.

STEP 02

Collect Information

Gather relevant information from approved passive or controlled sources.

STEP 03

Organize Findings

Record discovered information in a structured format so it can be reviewed later.

STEP 04

Validate

Check whether collected information is relevant, accurate and within the approved scope.

STEP 05

Prioritize

Identify information that may be useful for later authorized security assessment stages.

STEP 06

Document

Maintain clear notes and evidence for the assessment report.

06

Authorization & Scope

Reconnaissance is still a security activity. The fact that information is publicly accessible does not automatically mean that every form of testing or interaction is authorized.

⚠ AUTHORIZED ENVIRONMENT ONLY

Practice reconnaissance only against systems, domains, virtual machines, CTF targets or training environments for which you have explicit permission. Do not use reconnaissance techniques to investigate real organizations or individuals without authorization.

07

Professional Example

Imagine a company authorizes a security team to assess a dedicated training domain. Before deeper testing, the team collects information about the approved domain, its DNS configuration, documented technologies and intentionally exposed services.

AUTHORIZED RECONNAISSANCE

The security team records the information, verifies that every finding belongs to the approved scope and uses the resulting baseline to guide the next phase of the security assessment.

08

Key Terms

Passive Recon

Collecting information without directly interacting with the target system.

Active Recon

Gathering information through direct interaction with an authorized target.

OSINT

Open-source intelligence refers to collecting and analyzing information from publicly available sources.

Scope

The defined boundaries describing what may and may not be tested during an engagement.

Module Summary

Discover First. Understand the Target. Test Responsibly.

Reconnaissance is the information-discovery stage of ethical hacking. It helps security professionals build an organized understanding of an authorized target before deeper assessment activities begin. Effective reconnaissance depends on scope, accuracy, documentation and responsible use of information.
